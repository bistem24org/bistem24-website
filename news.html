Starting Off the Year with PhET in Nigeria

3/7/2023

0 Comments

 
PhET-certified teachers at Usra Academy.
​Building on her prior success hosting a BiSTEM24 PhET Virtual Workshop in mid-2022, PhET Fellow Rita Oladiran has expanded her work with a second cohort. With a workshop spanning from November 2022 into January 2023, a second cohort of teachers successfully completed the experience. The initial introductory session attracted over 180 educators, with organized teacher groups coming together in hubs, primarily from across northern Nigeria. One such hub included participants from Usra Academy, who were awarded certificates on February 15, 2023.
"The workshop was targeted toward using PhET resources and strategies to create a paradigm shift among participants: from the traditional didactic methods of transmitting knowledge to becoming learning facilitators, allowing and enabling the students to discover knowledge and develop a critical consciousness to question, investigate and reflect on science and the world around them." ~ Rita
Students work in a group with a simulation on a computer.
Each module started off with live workshop demonstrations with the participants over Zoom. The training program covered all 3 modules of the PhET professional development syllabus, starting with a general module “Introduction to PhET Simulations,” which provided an introductory overview to PhET simulations, and strategies for its use in active STEM teaching.
The second module “Activity Design for STEM with PhET”, was more practical and collaborative- particularly among the school groups, which further strengthened the participants’ understanding about student centered inquiry based learning. Much of the practical work in this section involved asynchronous learning, with collaborations among the school groups, peer interactions and feedback via social media.
In the final module, “Implementation of PhET Activities for STEM Education,” the teachers implemented their lessons integrating the PhET activities designed and employing active student centered inquiry methods, on their classrooms.
Children interact with the Natural Selection simulation.
Girls visualize how to make fractions of equal proportions. 1
The teachers commented on the impact of this experience with PhET, in their report and reflections at the final presentations in which they showed (digitally, with video presentation apps) how their students conducted their own investigations, created hypothesis, collected, analyzed and reported data digitally and with collaborative discussions. The live and asynchronous activities were designed to ensure that the teachers themselves first experienced and modeled these practices in collaborative discussions with colleagues and active engagement in planning, designing and facilitating group and individual lessons with informed considerations for adopting pedagogical approaches suited to their own particular contexts and lesson objectives using the PhET technology resources.
By focusing on the development of these essential skills, teachers prepare students to develop both conceptual and practical subject competence despite the unavailability of physical laboratories in secondary schools, and to excel in an increasingly digital world.

PhET as continuing professional development in nigeria

16/10/2022

0 Comments

 
​Rita leads an “Introduction to PhET Simulations” webinar session for teachers, along with colleague PhET Fellows Blessing Chukwuka (Nigeria), Akinola Olayinka (Nigeria), Godfrey Odhiambo (Kenya), Muhammad Chuka Joof (The Gambia), Cosmas Kathumba (Malawi), Adegboyega Adedirin (Namibia), Martha Mwiinga (Zambia), Dorothy Mensah (Ghana) and Richard Boni (Ghana). Credit: BiSTEM24 EdTech Foundation.


​Rita Oladiran is an educator who understands the power of collaboration. Her quest to seek educational change has been partially realized through the launch of her BiSTEM24 EdTech Foundation in Nigeria and is further bolstered by her engagement as a PhET Fellow.
Building on the PhET Virtual Workshops that were released in July 2022, Rita led an initiative to bring continuing professional development to teachers across Nigeria, reaching more than 350 educators, primarily from Nigeria, but also including individuals from Botswana, Ghana, Kenya, Morocco, Rwanda, and South Africa who heard about the initiative and requested to join. This major effort was undertaken in collaboration with the Kaduna State Ministry of Education, the 1 Million Teachers initiative, a network of private schools to which she has access, and multiple other PhET Fellows from across the continent.
​Rita was trained in a traditional African educational system, became a university-level engineering degree holder, and is now a STEM educator and EdTech specialist. She holds both a PGCE and a Digital master's qualification from the University of Nottingham, UK.







Rita shares the following:
"I experienced firsthand, the inadequacies of our science and math education in preparing our youth for the global 21st-century advancements. This became the main motivation for setting up the Bi-STEM24 Edtech Foundation–to break the barriers to quality STEM education toward building subject-competent and digitally confident future graduates.​ Thus, withthe activities undertaken with the PhET simulations, we hope to address some of the endemic problems Africa faces. Namely, much education here is theoretical (a result of a lack of lab equipment) and makes use of traditional method of teaching math and science. These traditional approaches do little to improve students’ understanding of concepts in these domains. Most importantly, traditional education does not promote active student-centered learning and engagement, as students often just passively ‘imbibe’ knowledge transmitted by their teachers, consequently promoting rote learning with adverse learning outcomes and a lack of knowledge transfer."
Due to common traditional classroom norms that African teachers are accustomed to in their practices, Rita made it her goal to support teachers regarding the awareness, possibilities, and benefits of establishing new norms of active student-centered learning with PhET simulations, and to assure them of support in achieving this.
To achieve this goal, starting in July 2022, she worked with collaborators to first present two short virtual introductory sessions on the use of PhET simulations, followed by the opportunity to engage in a much more extensive 30+ hour PhET Virtual Workshop. The introductory sessions expounded on the benefits of the PhET simulations and pedagogies for improving teachers’ instructional practice, to instill confidence in the use of the PhET simulations as virtual labs within the classroom.
The following Virtual Workshop involved a mix of self-paced asynchronous learning and exploration to allow the teachers to practice their own active learning and discovery of the PhET simulations. This all enabled them to design learning activities, select simulations for their activities vis-a-vis their proposed learning objectives, and create and implement their lesson plans with their students, choosing appropriate pedagogies adaptable to their peculiar context. More than 40 teachers successfully completed this process and shared a poster presentation of their lesson activities along with evidence of classroom implementation.
Rita’s initiative has been received well by the teachers who have participated in these activities, and school leaders are asking for more. “This is a welcome idea for schools that don’t have a good science lab,” shared Mr. Philip, a school owner in Nigeria. He told Rita, “I would like you to train my staff on it.” In response, Rita hopes to do at least two things as she moves into her 12-month leadership project for the PhET Fellowship: continue to offer continuing education to more teachers across the country and the region, and formally establish a tech-focused professional association to provide long-term support to teachers who want to make effective use of tools like PhET simulations.

0 Comments

 
Rita Oladiran speaks on 'making math and science learning easy with PhET Simulations' on this Twitter podcast.

Highlights of the discussion...
- The PhET Interactive Simulations (aka PhET sims) was funded from Prof. Carl Wieman- the founder's, Nobel prize winnings in 2002.
- PhET enables students develop agency and a growth mindset (vs a fixed mindset)
-  PhET simulations are more practicable in promoting equality and inclusion- due to its offline access, because of problems of low internet bandwidth and lack of infrastructure in most schools in Africa, when compared with the more imersive technology- VR & AR, at the primary and secondary school levels. 
- PhET sims are not designed to replace teaching, it's a power tool in the teacher's toolkit
- PhET sims are available to students for offline use on smartphones and tablets, at no cost.
- The sims content and principles are universally applicable to diff. curricular (science principles are universal)
- Teaching science in abstraction is hard. Today teachers are required to get creative.  learning technologies like PhET support STEM teaching and learning...
